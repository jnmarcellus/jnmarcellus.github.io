Staying Relevant as a Leader

I have been looking back over the last 12 years of my career, which seems odd, but I thought – I have already done much with my career in its front half. I am now setting my goals for the back half of my career because I want to accomplish some specific things before I am done.

  • Went from working on SharePoint for the State of Oklahoma to being a CIO for the 6th largest Blood Center in the US.
  • Project managed infrastructure for a 3 billion dollar divesture for one of largest natural gas companies in the country at the time.
  • Traded like a baseball player across three companies in roughly three years while doing different jobs at each stage, I loved the challenges at this time of my life.
  • With my teams, we built and remodeled several data centers.
  • Learned about Radio Tower Infrastructure
  • Built a team to launch an industry-focused Blood Establishment Computer System (BECs), which is like an EMR/ERP put together. Super proud of them!
  • Participated in three mergers and two divestitures, of those participated at the executive level for 3 of them
  • I was heartbroken when the company I sacrificed so much for was bought, but it worked out.
  • Started my own Managed Services Provider with my friends and, several years later, closed that up when one of them moved.
  • Helped design, and with my team, we built 70 mobile offices across three states
  • Rebuilt an entire IT Organization with amazing IT Leadership supporting the effort
  • Worked with two out of the big four accounting firms on major billion-dollar projects.
  • Started a monthly leadership breakfast with my friends
  • Mentored people across the globe to help reach their potential (this is one of my favorite items in my career)

I am proud of the teams and organizations I have served with over the years. I have learned a lot from my managers, directors, and CIOs that I served under, and I count myself lucky.

I am planning the back half of my career, which is weird because I am only 44; I don’t know many who think of it like this. The reality is we are lucky if we make it to retirement, and I want to make sure that I not only work on things that I enjoy but also benefit those in the community. Our time as leaders is finite and not a given; we can’t bank on having this opportunity forever.

  • Here is what I am working on to focus on for the next 3 – we will see how I do!
  • Continue to Grow Technically
  • Ratify the processes that others are missing in IT for how things connect.
  • Develop a better mentoring system for Managers and Directors.
  • Take on 2 Mentees a year at a manager/director level and help them grow.
  • Continue to build the breakfast into what it wants to be
  • Speak more often at conferences – the goal of at least 1 a year but maybe get up to 3
  • Be more involved in the community. I am not sure what this means just yet, but I shared with someone recently that I am continuing to think about serving at a local level in politics.

Overall, I am proud of my career and thankful for the opportunities I have been blessed with. I want to do a good job sharing those opportunities.
