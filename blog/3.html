Is Apple the new IBM? | Is Google the new Microsoft?

I posted this in May 23rd 2010 on my old blog, cleaned up the terrible grammar but otherwise I was pretty spot on with some of these predictions.

Why don’t we take a trip down memory lane to the 80s? In the 80s, there were significant technical expansions in the computer market. You had the trendy Apple II, four different generations of processors, Big Blue, and a lot of Software we use today was born in that decade. In that decade, Apple tried to set itself as the game changer, later failing and splitting with its founders, only to be reborn in the later 90s. It was safe to say the dominant player in the 80s was IBM, as they had the hardware and software market cornered, and everyone thought you would be crazy if IBM were not a significant player ten years later. Now that IBM has recently sold its consumer production PC line to a Chinese manufacturer and is only now realizing it is Software that drives hardware, they have a long time to return to where they were. In the late ’80s into the early ’90s, a quickly growing company called Microsoft Soft pushed its Software by getting the best applications on it, which in turn drove hardware sales to run the Software.
In many cases, Microsoft sacrificed cost in the short term, knowing the vast gains in market share in a long time. It seems 15-20 years later, we are seeing the same thing again. Ironically, Apple is IBM in this case, locking down and controlling its hardware and Software similar to what IBM did. Releasing failed and irrelevant products due to lack of vision, Apple’s iTV, and the newest useless product, the iPad. There is a train coming, ladies and gentlemen. It is called Android, and it is not slowing down in the least bit, with Google’s recently announced OS – Chrome- to its launch of the Nexus One hardware. Google is subsidizing its current projects with its vast treasure chest of capital to position itself as a major player in 3-4 years.
Recent polls show Apple’s market share rose 112% from last year’s first quarter, but it also shows that Google’s Android OS share rose 806%. You want to know why because carriers see that they can use this free OS with semi-standardized hardware and roll out phones that used to take 2-3 years to design. They can do it in half of that time. Plus, developing applications on Android is accessible to developers, who can make money off of it as well. The carriers still make money because the outrageous data plans cost more than a landline connection with 3x the capacity. Recent statistics have shown the rate of applications being released on Android’s Market has increased 600% from last year, according to androlib.com. The software will always drive hardware; there is nothing to consider. Apple has lost sight of that, letting their OS-X platform and PC hardware become second seat. Ballmer said in a recent interview that there are three different platforms you need to develop simultaneously: Small, Medium, and Large form factors. To create for one without the other leaves you in a position that Microsoft is now fixing with its’ mobile line launch of Win 7 and Kin. These are likely not game changes, with the recent reviews being positive but not overwhelming. Google recently announced partnering with Intel, Apple’s other Ex-Girlfriend, to develop a competing product to Apple’s failed TV program and Microsoft’s quasi-TV solution in the Xbox. We will not see any Google TV boxes but embedded hardware/software solutions for TVs in the next 3-4 years.
With Apple, I give them about 5-6 years, and they will lose their luster. Google is just getting started.
